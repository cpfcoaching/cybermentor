Submitted Track
The Collaborative Partner

CyberMentor is an interactive expert that guides users through one of the most daunting career transitions they'll face. It asks the right questions, remembers everything it learns across sessions, and evolves with each interaction — the definition of a collaborative partner with memory.

The Problem

Breaking in is hard. The advice is contradictory. The path is invisible.

Aspiring security professionals face four compounding obstacles: overwhelming information, generic study plans, no feedback on resumes, and expensive interview coaching. CyberMentor solves all four.

😵‍💫
Information Overload
Reddit says OSCP first. YouTube says Security+ is useless. LinkedIn says you need a degree. The signal-to-noise ratio is brutal.
📋
Generic Study Plans
"Read this book" doesn't account for whether you have 5 hours a week or 25, or what role you're actually targeting.
📄
Resume in a Void
Most people submit dozens of applications before getting feedback. By then, months are wasted.
🎤
Interview Prep Costs Money
Mock interviews with real professionals are expensive. Practicing alone provides zero feedback on answer quality.
What It Does

Five coaching capabilities, one persistent agent.

Every capability is implemented as a custom Antigravity SDK tool — independently callable, well-documented, and autonomously invoked by Gemini based on user context.

🗺️
Career Path Advisor
Maps your background and goals to the right cybersecurity career track. SOC, Pen Test, GRC, Cloud Security, or CISO — with real salary ranges and time-to-hire data.
📅
Study Planner
Week-by-week study schedule calibrated to your hours per week and experience level. Four phases: foundations → domains → practice exams → review.
📄
Resume Analyzer
Scores your resume out of 100. Identifies missing certs, unnamed tools, unquantified achievements, and missing cyber-specific keywords.
🎤
Interview Coach
Role-specific technical and behavioral questions with rubric-based scoring. Identifies exactly which key points you missed and provides model answer templates.
🧠
Persistent Memory
Every goal, milestone, and session is stored in Firestore. Returning users are recognized and coached based on prior context — no re-introduction required.
🎬
Visual Role Previews
Google Veo generates short cinematic clips showing what different cybersecurity roles look like day-to-day. "Show me what a SOC analyst does" → video.

Findings & Learnings

What building this taught me.

01
Tool docstrings are your routing logic
The "Use this tool when..." section of each function docstring is the most important code in the project. Gemini routes to the correct tool based on docstring semantics — no custom router needed. Write better docstrings, not routing code.
02
Two-tier inference: Gemma for classification, Gemini for generation
Not every inference call needs Gemini. Intent classification, resume skill extraction, and readiness scoring are structured, low-complexity tasks where Gemma 3 is faster and cheaper. This pattern is worth adopting broadly.
03
Streaming changes perceived quality more than content does
SSE token streaming makes the agent feel thoughtful and alive. Users rated identical content dramatically higher when it streamed versus appeared all at once. The animation of text arriving creates the impression of active intelligence.
04
Persona files are underrated
A detailed persona.txt (tone, scope, refusal criteria, response structure) produced far more consistent outputs than a short system prompt. Specifying "always end with a next step" and "never make users feel bad for not knowing something" transformed the agent's character.
05
Graceful degradation is non-negotiable
Every external dependency (Firestore, Veo, Lyria, Gemma) has a local fallback. The agent never breaks — it degrades gracefully and explains what would happen if the cloud service were available. This made local development and demos dramatically smoother.
